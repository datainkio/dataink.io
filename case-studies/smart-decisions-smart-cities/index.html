Product & Platform

Smart Decisions, Smart Cities

CityForward was an innovative web platform developed by IBM as part of its Smarter Planet Initiative. Designed to serve city officials, researchers, academics, and engaged citizens, CityForward offered a comprehensive view of urban life across 250 major cities globally. The platform aimed to facilitate data-driven decision-making and foster a deeper understanding of urban trends.

Overview

01 / Problem
City Forward was IBM's free public platform for exploring, charting and sharing city data, built on nine IBM products. Its data came in as OLAP cubes, and five weeks before launch the plan was 47% complete.
02 / Design Challenge
Russ worked where the specification met the build: integrating the home and exploration pages and carrying design intent into the implementation.
03 / Approach
To hold the launch date, the team cut twenty features, each with a one-line reason. The 2011–12 redesign concepts shifted the site's language from the tool toward what people do with it: explore, collaborate, tell stories.
04 / Outcome
A public beta was live by September 2010. The March 2011 launch date comes from IBM's public materials, not the archive, which holds no metrics or feedback.

Process + Evidence Plates

    • Fig 01 / Release Planning

    Decision: Cut twenty features before launch, each with a one-line reason

    Result: The launch date held, and every cut was traceable

    • Fig 02 / Information Architecture

    Decision: Version the IA document rather than redraw it

    Result: Forty revisions stayed traceable, and the spec became a record of what shipped

    • Fig 03 / Content Strategy

    Decision: Reframe the top level from the tool to what people do: explore, collaborate, learn

    Result: Data cubes read as an invitation to curiosity, not a database

    • Fig 04 / Interaction Design

    Decision: Use the empty state to show what other people had made

    Result: First-time visitors had a reason and a prompt to create their own exploration

    • Fig 05 / Data Analysis & Visualization

    Decision: Make the map the primary canvas for city data

    Result: People started from a place they knew rather than a table

    • Fig 06 / Digital Strategy

    Decision: Make the platform free to use

    Result: A global audience could work with urban data, not only those who could pay for it

    • Fig 07 / Information Architecture
    • Fig 07 / Data Analysis & Visualization

    Decision: Gather city data scattered across government sites into one source

    Result: Education, safety, health and transport statistics could be explored in one place and one format

    • Fig 08 / Knowledge Sharing

    Decision: Let users make visualizations and share them with a community

    Result: City data became a conversation in which people exchanged ideas about urban problems

    • Fig 09 / Data Analysis & Visualization
    • Fig 09 / Experience Design

    Decision: Keep the exploration tools simple enough for anyone

    Result: Non-specialists found patterns, trends and correlations and took them to the community

    • Fig 010 / Content Strategy

    Decision: Give users tools to build stories from city data

    Result: Users made data-based narratives that could shape policy and public perception

    • Fig 011 / Data Analysis & Visualization

    Decision: Put cities side by side on the same measures

    Result: Users saw patterns and trends across cities that offered new perspectives on urban policy

    • Fig 012 / User Flow Analysis

    Decision: Join data access, exploration and storytelling in one workflow

    Result: Users went from finding data to making a case without leaving the platform
