Overview

01 / Problem
James Zabiela is a British DJ known for experimenting with live performance and technology, and his fans were already posting photos, tweets and video of him across the web. His 2009 site had to bring that in.
02 / Design Challenge
As Bluecadet's Lead Developer, Russ designed the interactions and built the site on Drupal, working from the visual designers' mockups.
03 / Approach
Fan photos from Flickr, tweets and YouTube videos flowed into the site through a moderation queue. A persistent audio player kept music running between pages, with the mailing-list sign-up inside it.
04 / Outcome
Captures from March 2009 show the site live; the case study's 2011 SXSW award needs checking against that date. No traffic data survives.

Process + Evidence Plates

    • Fig 01 / Content Strategy

    Decision: Pull fans' Flickr photos, tweets and YouTube videos into the official site

    Result: The site was a living canvas for the community already gathering around James

    • Fig 02 / Content Management

    Decision: Aggregation + Moderation

    Result: The editorial queue let fan-generated content flow in without James or his team losing editorial control

    • Fig 03 / Interaction Design

    Decision: Persistent beats

    Result: The design strategy called for a cinematic flow between views. The site's audio player was core to that film experience. James' tracks brought continuity to visitors' exploration.

    • Fig 04 / Digital Strategy

    Decision: Offer banners and assets for fans to publish

    Result: Fans became a distribution channel

    • Fig 05 / Interaction Design

    Decision: A motion strategy set to James' music

    Result: Intro, idle and outro sequences for every page transition. Visitors felt they were inside the DJ booth.

    • Fig 06 / Content Management

    Decision: Easy adoption

    Result: Integration with Twitter let James keep the site current with a habit he already had, not a new one.
