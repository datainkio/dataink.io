An Artist's Process

An interactive and immersive website dedicated to the life and work of the legendary western painter Robert Lougheed.

Clients/Partners
Bluecadet, National Museum of Wildlife Art
Roles
Lead Developer
Industry
Museums & Memorials
Deliverables
Information Visualization , Development , Interaction Design

Overview

01 / Problem
Robert Lougheed (1910–1982) was a Western and wildlife illustrator whose life had gone largely untold. The National Museum of Western Art in Jackson Hole wanted a kiosk and web experience about his work.
02 / Design Challenge
As Bluecadet's Lead Developer, Russ designed the interactions and built the piece, working from the visual designers' mockups.
03 / Approach
The interface stands in for his studio: artwork on an easel, torn paper tags as navigation, the room out of focus behind. A timeline tells his life, and his pulp covers and commissions sit beside the gallery work.
04 / Outcome
Captures of the finished piece survive; no launch or visitor data does.

Process + Evidence Plates

    • Fig 01 / Graphic Design

    Decision: The Artist's Perspective

    Result: An interface designed so visitors feel they are seeing the work as Lougheed saw it.

    • Fig 02 / Interaction Design

    Decision: Use motion to carry the story from one section to the next

    Result: Transitions kept the feeling of flow and exploration unbroken between sections

    • Fig 03 / Content Strategy

    Decision: A biography designed for exploration

    Result: The timeline format maintained continuity while the design encouraged free-form exploration of Lougheed's life.

    • Fig 04 / Information Architecture

    Decision: Catalogue pulp covers and commissions beside the gallery work

    Result: His illustration read as a body of work, not a list of jobs

    • Fig 05 / Content Strategy

    Decision: Show the painting process from sketch to finished work

    Result: Visitors understood how the paintings were made
