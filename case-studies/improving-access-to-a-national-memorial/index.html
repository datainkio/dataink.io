Improving Access to a National Memorial

Bluecadet created an interactive website for the Lincoln Memorial, commissioned by the National Park Service. The project gave visitors everywhere a virtual yet deeply engaging experience of the memorial, emphasizing accessibility and historical reverence.

Clients/Partners
National Park Service, Bluecadet
Roles

Industry

Recognition (Summary)

Overview

01 / Problem
The National Park Service wanted people who couldn't visit the Lincoln Memorial to experience it anyway. The piece combined spherical panoramas with video and stills.
02 / Design Challenge
As Bluecadet's Lead Developer, Russ designed the interactions and built the piece, working from the visual designers' mockups.
03 / Approach
The whole interface sits inside a photograph of the March on Washington crowd, so the memorial's civil-rights meaning comes first. Panoramas carry hotspots with interpretive notes, the videos have captions, and there's an HTML fallback.
04 / Outcome
Captures of the finished piece and its hand-drawn hotspot spec survive; no visitor data does.

Process + Evidence Plates

    • Fig 01 / Content Strategy

    Decision: Set the whole interface inside a photograph of the March on Washington

    Result: The memorial's civil-rights meaning came first, before a word of content

    • Fig 02 / Accessibility Standards & Best Practices

    Decision: Caption the videos and provide an HTML fallback

    Result: People who couldn't hear the audio or run Flash could still visit

    • Fig 03 / Interaction Design

    Decision: Place interpretive hotspots inside the panoramas

    Result: Remote visitors explored details they might miss in person

    • Fig 04 / Content Strategy

    Decision: Pair named rangers and contributors with themes

    Result: The memorial was interpreted by people, not anonymous captions

    • Fig 05 / Information Architecture

    Decision: Cross-link panoramas and reflections rather than separate them

    Result: Visitors could move between place and meaning freely

    • Fig 06 / Content Strategy
    • Fig 06 / Experience Design

    Decision: Combine video, still photography and panoramas in one tour

    Result: Visitors who couldn't travel to Washington got a full experience of the memorial

    • Fig 07 / Experience Design

    Decision: Shoot spherical panoramas from 21 vantage points

    Result: Virtual visitors could walk around the memorial with a sense of being there

    • Fig 08 / Content Strategy

    Decision: Score the rangers' reflections with archival footage and music

    Result: Personal narratives made an emotional connection to the memorial's history

    • Fig 09 / Experience Design

    Decision: Let the content speak instead of the technology

    Result: Historical events and figures felt relatable and tangible, with no gimmicks in the way
