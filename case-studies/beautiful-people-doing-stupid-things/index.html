Advertising

Beautiful People Doing Stupid Things

As part of the largest and most integrated global marketing campaign in Columbia's history, the Omni-Heat design emphasized cinematic seamlessness as users navigated through product shots and videos of incredibly attractive people engaging in humorous, exaggerated activities.

Clients/Partners
Columbia Sportswear, Transistor Studios
Roles
Rich Internet Application Developer
Industry
Advertising
Deliverables
Bilingual Content Strategy , Development , Wireframing

Overview

01 / Problem
Columbia Sportswear launched Omni-Heat, a reflective thermal lining, with a campaign by Butler, Shine, Stern & Partners and visual design by Transistor Studios. The microsite had to open in the US and six international markets at once.
02 / Design Challenge
Russ designed the interaction and experience, joining Transistor's visuals to Columbia's content, and did the Flash development.
03 / Approach
The site opens by detecting location: US visitors go straight in, and everyone else meets a language gate headed "The language of cold is universal." The preloader carries the product's pitch instead of a spinner.
04 / Outcome
Wireframes, flow and specs delivered 1 June 2010, and the build was tested in IE7. It was tagged for DoubleClick, but no results survive; Columbia's sales growth that season is brand-level, as the case study says.

Process + Evidence Plates

    • Fig 01 / Content Strategy

    Decision: Write the country and language gate in the campaign's voice

    Result: The most functional screen in the site still sounded like the campaign

    • Fig 02 / User Flow Analysis

    Decision: Detect location first and send US visitors straight in

    Result: One launch served the US and six international markets without extra steps for most visitors

    • Fig 03 / Interaction Design

    Decision: Carry navigation and the product pitch in the preloader

    Result: Load time became part of the story instead of dead time

    • Fig 04 / Data Analysis & Visualization

    Decision: Present each cold-weather stunt as an instrument readout

    Result: The product's claim read as evidence, with location, temperature and altitude attached

    • Fig 05 / Development

    Decision: Test the build across browsers down to IE7

    Result: The campaign worked on the browsers its audience actually used

    • Fig 06 / Interaction Design

    Decision: Match absurd content with equally over-the-top interactions

    Result: Rotating globes, creeping ice ages and giant freezers kept the story and the joke moving

    • Fig 07 / Digital Strategy

    Decision: Design the site as one channel in a multichannel campaign

    Result: The web experience introduced Omni-Heat in step with print, social and live events
